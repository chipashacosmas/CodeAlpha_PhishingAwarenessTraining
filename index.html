MODULE 01

Taxonomy of Social Engineering

Explore the diverse spectrum of deception techniques used to manipulate psychological triggers.

  • Spear Phishing: Highly customized attacks targeting specific individuals using OSINT reconnaissance.
  • Whaling (CEO Fraud): Deceptive targeting of C-level executives or authorized financial signers.
  • Smishing & Vishing: SMS-based payload links and voice/AI-generated deepfake social engineering.
  • Clone Phishing: Duplicating a legitimate prior email and replacing clean links with malicious payloads.
  • Business Email Compromise (BEC): Account takeover or domain spoofing to initiate fraudulent wire transfers.
🧠
MODULE 02

Psychological Attack Vectors

Threat actors do not hack systems first; they hack human cognition. Understand the emotional levers.

  • Artificial Urgency: Tight deadlines ("Action required within 1 hour or account suspended").
  • Authority Impersonation: Posing as IT Administrators, Legal counsel, or the CEO.
  • Fear & Panic: Fabricated security alerts, missed court summons, or tax audit warnings.
  • Greed & Opportunity: Unexpected bonuses, lottery payouts, or cryptocurrency investments.
  • Curiosity & Helpfulness: Shipping package tracking or internal company organizational changes.
🔍
MODULE 03

Email Anatomy & Header Forensics

Dissect technical headers and detect deceptive indicators invisible in the formatted body.

  • Display Name Spoofing: Displaying a trusted name while the actual sender mailbox is external.
  • Reply-To Mismatch: Directing responses to an attacker-controlled drop address.
  • Email Authentication Triad: Verifying SPF (Sender Policy Framework), DKIM (cryptographic signature), and DMARC policy enforcement.
  • Lookalike Domains (Typosquatting): Registering deceptive variations (e.g. rnicrosoft.com or paypa1.com).
  • Dangerous Attachments: Obfuscated files, macro-enabled documents (.xlsm), and ISO disk images.
🌐
MODULE 04

Deceptive URL Anatomy & Evasion

Master how attackers manipulate URLs to deceive security filters and human eyes.

  • Subdomain Manipulation: login.microsoft.com.attacker-server.net
  • Open Redirect Exploitation: Abusing clean domains to forward visitors to credential harvesters.
  • Internationalized Domain Names (IDN) Homoglyphs: Swapping Latin characters with visually identical Cyrillic glyphs.
  • URL Shorteners & Obfuscation: Hiding payloads behind bit.ly, tinyurl, or encoded IP decimals.
  • Reverse Proxy Phishing (AitM): Evilginx-style frameworks capturing active session cookies past MFA.

Catastrophic Real-World Phishing Breaches

In-depth analysis of major cyber incidents that began with a single deceptive message.

2016 BEC / CEO FRAUD

FACC Aerospace — $61M CEO Fraud

An Austrian manufacturer of Boeing and Airbus components fell victim to a Business Email Compromise attack. Attackers spoofed the email address of the CEO and instructed an entry-level accounting officer to transfer funds for an alleged "secret acquisition."

Key Failure: Absence of dual-control out-of-band phone verification for high-value treasury transfers.
2021 CREDENTIAL HARVESTING

Colonial Pipeline — Single Password Breach

DarkSide ransomware operators shut down 5,500 miles of vital fuel pipeline on the US East Coast. The breach began when attackers compromised a single legacy VPN account whose credentials were leaked via an employee phishing campaign lacking Multi-Factor Authentication.

Key Failure: Legacy perimeter services operating without enforced FIDO2 or app-based Multi-Factor Authentication.
2022 MFA BYPASS / SMISHING

Twilio & Uber — MFA Fatigue & Smishing

The "0ktapus" threat group targeted over 130 organizations using SMS phishing (smishing) disguised as IT password reset notifications. When users encountered MFA, attackers deployed MFA push-bombing (fatigue) until tired employees accepted the push prompt.

Key Failure: Reliance on simple push notifications instead of phishing-resistant FIDO2 Passkeys or number matching.

Standard Operating Procedure: Phishing Incident Escalation

Actionable security protocol for security operations teams and employees when suspicious activity occurs.

01

Immediate Isolation & Containment

If you clicked a link or opened an attachment:

  • Disconnect your system from Wi-Fi and unplug the Ethernet network cable immediately.
  • Do not power down or restart the computer (preserves volatile RAM evidence for forensic capture).
  • Notify the Security Operations Center (SOC) or IT Security on-call personnel via an alternate channel (phone/in-person).
02

Credential Invalidation & Revocation

If corporate credentials were typed into an external web page:

  • Immediately initiate an enterprise session revocation and password reset from a known-clean device.
  • Audit all active OAuth application permissions and enrolled MFA authenticator devices for unauthorized additions.
  • Examine Exchange / Google Workspace mail forwarding rules for unauthorized inbox routing rules.
03

Header Extraction & Threat Hunting

Extracting the raw Internet headers to protect colleagues:

  • Export the message as an .eml or .msg RFC 822 file attachment (forwarding as plain text strips crucial cryptographic headers).
  • SOC extracts the originating server IP, SPF/DKIM verification status, and malicious URLs.
  • Add malicious URLs and sender domains to enterprise boundary firewall, secure email gateway (SEG), and DNS blackholes.
